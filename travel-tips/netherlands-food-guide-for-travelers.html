A Netherlands food guide for travelers, starting in Amsterdam

Amsterdam's food is more varied than its snack stereotypes. Dutch comfort food sits next to Indonesian, Surinamese and modern European cooking shaped by the country's history.

Four areas to eat in

The Jordaan

West of the centre, the canal-side Jordaan has brown cafés, bistros and small restaurants.

De Pijp

South of the centre, De Pijp around the Albert Cuyp Market is lively, with food from around the world.

Amsterdam Oost

East of the centre, a multicultural area with neighbourhood restaurants and a more local feel.

Noord

Across the IJ by free ferry, Amsterdam Noord has converted industrial spaces, food halls and waterside restaurants.

Good to knowRijsttafel, "rice table", reflects the Netherlands' colonial history in Indonesia. It is a sit-down feast best shared by a group.

Beyond Dutch food

Surinamese and Indonesian restaurants are an important part of Amsterdam's food scene. A Surinamese roti or a broodje from a takeaway counter makes a cheap, filling lunch.

Cards are the default in Amsterdam, and some places do not accept cash at all.

Common questions

What is rijsttafel?

A Dutch-Indonesian meal of many small dishes served with rice, best shared.

How do I get to Amsterdam Noord?

Free ferries cross the IJ from behind Amsterdam Centraal station.

Do Amsterdam restaurants accept cash?

Many prefer cards, and some are card-only.
