Where to eat in Miami without wasting your trip

Miami is several food cultures in one city: Cuban cooking, Caribbean and Latin American neighbourhoods, seafood and contemporary restaurants, each in its own pocket. Plan by neighbourhood to avoid long drives between meals.

eSIMCircle Editorial · Published November 15, 2025 · Updated November 15, 2025 · 3 min read

Four neighbourhoods

Little Havana

Along Calle Ocho, Cuban restaurants, bakeries and ventanitas (walk-up windows) serve the classics. Start here.

Wynwood

The arts district has trendy restaurants, food halls and food trucks.

South Beach

Beachfront dining, from casual to expensive. Be wary of tourist-trap prices on the busiest strips.

Coconut Grove

A leafy, older neighbourhood with relaxed waterfront and neighbourhood restaurants.

What to order

DishWhat it is
Cuban sandwichRoast pork, ham, Swiss cheese, pickles and mustard pressed on Cuban bread
Frita cubanaA seasoned Cuban burger topped with shoestring potatoes
Stone crab clawsA South Florida speciality, served chilled with mustard sauce, in season
CevicheRaw fish cured in citrus, common in Miami's Latin American restaurants
Ropa viejaShredded beef in tomato sauce with rice and beans
Key lime pieA tart South Florida classic
Good to knowStone crab season runs roughly from mid-October to May. Outside it, the claws are not available.

Tipping

In the US, tipping at sit-down restaurants is expected, usually a percentage of the bill. At counters, card screens often show a tip prompt; check whether it is optional before confirming.

Getting around

Miami is spread out. Group meals by neighbourhood and use rideshare between them. Check live traffic before setting off at busy times.

Common questions

Where is the best Cuban food in Miami?

Little Havana, along Calle Ocho, is the heart of Miami's Cuban food.

When is stone crab season?

Roughly mid-October to May.

Do I need to tip in Miami restaurants?

At sit-down restaurants, tipping is expected. At counters, tip prompts are usually optional.
