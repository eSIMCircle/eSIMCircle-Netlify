A UAE food guide for travelers, starting in Abu Dhabi

The most rewarding food days in Abu Dhabi mix Emirati cooking with the city's Gulf, Levantine and Indian influences, instead of staying inside hotel restaurants.

Four areas to eat in

Al Mina and the port area

Around the Mina Zayed port, markets and casual restaurants include seafood places close to the fish market.

The Corniche and Al Zahiyah

The waterfront and the central district behind it have cafés, Lebanese and Indian restaurants, and places to eat with sea views.

Muroor and Mushrif

These residential districts have neighbourhood restaurants where locals eat, including Emirati and Gulf cooking.

Yas Island

Home to theme parks and leisure venues, Yas has many restaurants, mostly in hotels and complexes.

Good to knowEmirati restaurants are fewer than you might expect in a city full of international food. Seek one out for at least one meal.

Local customs

During Ramadan, eating in public during daylight hours is restricted for everyone; check current guidance. Many restaurants serve iftar after sunset, which can be a special time to eat out. Alcohol is mainly served in licensed hotel venues.

Getting around

Abu Dhabi is spread out, so taxis are the easiest way between areas. A working map helps you share your location with the driver.

Common questions

What is machboos?

Spiced rice with meat or fish and dried lime, one of the core Emirati dishes.

Can I eat in public during Ramadan in Abu Dhabi?

Eating in public during daylight hours is restricted. Check current guidance before your trip.

Where is alcohol served in Abu Dhabi?

Mainly in licensed venues, usually in hotels.
