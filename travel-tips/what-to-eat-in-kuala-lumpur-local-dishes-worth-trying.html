What to eat in Kuala Lumpur: local dishes worth trying

Kuala Lumpur is easiest to understand through its three great food traditions: Malay, Chinese and Indian. A good first trip deliberately includes all three.

eSIMCircle Editorial · Published October 6, 2025 · Updated August 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Eat from all three traditions

Malay

Nasi lemak

Coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts, egg and cucumber, often with fried chicken or rendang. Malaysia's national dish, eaten at breakfast or any time.

Chinese

Char kway teow

Wok-fried flat rice noodles with a smoky edge.

Hokkien mee

Kuala Lumpur's own style: thick noodles braised in dark soy, rich and savoury. A late-night favourite.

Bak kut teh

Peppery or herbal pork-rib soup with rice and tea, often eaten in the morning.

Indian

Roti canai

Flaky griddled bread with dhal or curry, cheap and good at breakfast. Watch it being stretched and flipped.

Banana-leaf rice

Rice with vegetables, curries and sides served South Indian style on a banana leaf. Eat with your right hand, as many locals do, or ask for cutlery.

Good to knowFold the banana leaf towards you when you finish. It is a sign that you enjoyed the meal.

Common questions

What is the national dish of Malaysia?

Nasi lemak, coconut rice with sambal and sides.

Where is Little India in Kuala Lumpur?

In Brickfields, known for banana-leaf rice and Indian food.

What is KL-style Hokkien mee?

Thick noodles braised in dark soy sauce, a Kuala Lumpur speciality.
