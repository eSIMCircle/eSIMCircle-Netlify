Where to eat in Melaka without wasting your trip

Melaka's food is inseparable from its history: Malay, Chinese, Peranakan and Portuguese-Eurasian kitchens, side by side. The most rewarding trip deliberately samples more than one tradition.

Four places to eat

Jonker and Heeren Street

The heart of the old town, with Peranakan restaurants, chicken rice ball shops and cafés. Busy, especially on weekends.

Ujong Pasir

A neighbourhood south of the centre known for seafood and local Malay cooking.

The Portuguese Settlement

Home to Melaka's Portuguese-Eurasian community, where seafood restaurants and Eurasian dishes such as devil curry can be found.

Klebang and Batu Berendam

Outside the centre, Klebang is known for coconut shakes and seaside snacks.

Practical notes

Popular chicken rice ball and cendol shops get long queues at weekends; go early. Carry cash for small places, and use Grab to reach Ujong Pasir or Klebang.

Common questions

What are chicken rice balls?

Hainanese chicken rice shaped into compact balls, a Melaka speciality.

What is satay celup?

Skewers dipped into a shared pot of hot peanut sauce, cooked at the table.

Where can I try Portuguese-Eurasian food in Melaka?

At the Portuguese Settlement, home to the community and its restaurants.
