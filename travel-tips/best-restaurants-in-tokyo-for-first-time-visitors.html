The best restaurants in Tokyo for first-time visitors

Tokyo has more restaurants than anyone can try, so chasing a list is a losing game. Let your sightseeing route choose the neighbourhood, then pick one or two dishes worth seeking out there.

Four neighbourhoods, four kinds of meal

Shinjuku

Ramen counters, yakitori alleys and department-store restaurants around one enormous station. Good for a casual dinner.

Shibuya and Harajuku

Younger and trendier, with cafés, casual dining and chains popular with locals.

Ginza and the Tsukiji area

Ginza has some of Tokyo's most refined dining, including sushi counters. The Tsukiji outer market nearby is a morning destination for seafood and snacks.

Asakusa and Kanda

Old Tokyo, around Sensō-ji temple, with long-established tempura, soba and sukiyaki restaurants.

Good to knowMany of Tokyo's best restaurants offer set lunches at a fraction of their dinner prices. Make lunch your splurge meal.

Ordering tips

Some ramen and casual restaurants use ticket machines at the entrance: you choose and pay first, then hand the ticket over. Tipping is not expected in Japan.

Reservations

High-end sushi and kaiseki restaurants often need bookings well ahead, sometimes through a hotel concierge. Casual places rarely take them; expect to queue at popular ramen shops.

Common questions

Do I need to tip in Tokyo restaurants?

No. Tipping is not expected in Japan.

How do ticket machines work in ramen shops?

You choose and pay at the machine by the entrance, then hand the ticket to the staff.

Is lunch cheaper than dinner in Tokyo?

Often, yes. Many restaurants offer set lunches at much lower prices than dinner.
