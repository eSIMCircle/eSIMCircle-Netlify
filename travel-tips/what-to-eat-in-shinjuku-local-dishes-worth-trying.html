What to eat in Shinjuku: local dishes worth trying

Shinjuku is one of the easiest places in Tokyo for a complete food day. Ramen counters, yakitori alleys, department-store restaurants and serious dining all surround the same enormous station.

Six dishes to look for

Ramen

Shinjuku has a deep concentration of specialist ramen counters, from long-running shops to new names. Many use ticket machines at the door.

Yakitori

Grilled chicken skewers, served at the tiny counters of Omoide Yokocho and in izakayas across the district.

Tonkatsu

Breaded pork cutlet with rice, shredded cabbage and miso soup. An easy, filling sit-down meal.

Sushi

From conveyor-belt restaurants to formal counters, Shinjuku covers every price level.

Japanese curry

Fast, cheap and comforting, ideal on a busy transit day.

Tempura

As a tendon rice bowl for a quick lunch, or as a set meal.

Good to knowDepartment stores near the station have restaurant floors on their upper levels. They are a good fallback when the streets are crowded.

What meals cost

Before publication, a quick casual meal was about ¥1,000 to ¥2,000, a comfortable sit-down meal ¥2,000 to ¥5,000, and a premium meal ¥8,000 or more.

Practical notes

Save each restaurant with its nearest station exit. Some small counters prefer cash. Tipping is not expected.

Common questions

Where can I eat yakitori in Shinjuku?

At the small counters of Omoide Yokocho, near the west side of the station.

What is tonkatsu?

Breaded pork cutlet, usually served with rice, cabbage and miso soup.

Do Shinjuku restaurants take cards?

Larger ones do. Small counters may prefer cash.
