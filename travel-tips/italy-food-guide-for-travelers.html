An Italy food guide for travelers, focused on Venice

Venice rewards travelers who step one or two streets away from the obvious squares. Build meals around lagoon seafood, cicchetti and simple pasta rather than generic "Italian" menus.

Four areas to eat in

Cannaregio

The northern district has canal-side restaurants and bacari where many locals eat, especially along the fondamenta.

San Polo

Around the Rialto market, San Polo is packed with bacari and trattorias, best for cicchetti.

Dorsoduro

South of the Grand Canal, a student and residential area with relaxed restaurants and bars.

Castello

The eastern district, away from San Marco, has quieter neighbourhood restaurants.

What to order

DishWhat it is
Sarde in saorSweet-sour sardines with onions, vinegar and raisins
Bigoli in salsaThick pasta with onion and anchovy sauce
Baccalà mantecatoWhipped salt cod, on bread or polenta
Risotto al nero di seppiaRisotto black with cuttlefish ink
Fegato alla venezianaCalf's liver with slow-cooked onions and polenta
CicchettiSmall bites at a bar, with seafood, meat or vegetables
Good to knowSarde in saor began as a way to preserve fish on long sea voyages. It is now one of Venice's best-loved starters.

Avoiding tourist traps

Be wary of restaurants with menus in many languages and staff calling you in from the street, especially near San Marco. Walk a few minutes away and look for places full of Italian speakers.

Beyond Venice

Across Italy, each region has its own dishes. Eat the local specialities wherever you are, and avoid places that offer every famous Italian dish on one menu.

Common questions

What should I eat in Venice?

Lagoon seafood dishes such as sarde in saor, cuttlefish-ink risotto and baccalà mantecato, plus cicchetti.

How do I avoid tourist-trap restaurants in Venice?

Walk away from the main squares and avoid places with multilingual menus and street touts.

Is there a cover charge in Venice restaurants?

Often. A coperto and sometimes a service charge should be listed on the menu.
