What to eat in Singapore's Chinatown: local dishes worth trying

Chinatown is ideal for a dish-by-dish food day. Hawker centres, roast-meat shops and old Chinese restaurants are all within walking distance, so you can graze without planning transport.

Six dishes to look for

Hainanese chicken rice

Poached or roasted chicken with rice cooked in chicken stock, served with chilli and ginger sauces. Maxwell Food Centre is known for it.

Char kway teow

Wok-fried flat rice noodles with dark soy, egg and savoury toppings. Look for the smoky edge of a hot wok.

Bak chor mee

Minced-meat noodles with vinegar, chilli and mushrooms, served dry or as soup.

Roast meats

Char siu, roast pork and roast duck over rice. Chinatown's roast-meat shops hang their meats in the window.

Yong tau foo

Tofu and vegetables stuffed with fish paste. You choose the pieces, then have them served dry or in soup.

Oyster cake

A crisp savoury fritter with oysters and minced meat, found at traditional snack stalls.

Good to knowAt yong tau foo stalls, you pick your own items from the display. Point, pay, and say whether you want soup or dry.

What meals cost

Before publication, a quick hawker meal cost about S$5 to S$12, a comfortable sit-down meal S$15 to S$35, and a premium meal S$60 or more.

Common questions

Where can I eat chicken rice in Chinatown?

Maxwell Food Centre is especially known for it.

How does yong tau foo work?

You choose your own stuffed tofu and vegetables, then have them served dry or in soup.

Do hawker stalls sell out?

Popular stalls can sell out, so go earlier in the day.
