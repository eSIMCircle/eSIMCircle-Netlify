A France food guide for travelers, starting in Bordeaux

Bordeaux's cooking is shaped by south-west French produce, Atlantic seafood and the wine country around it. You do not need a luxury restaurant to eat regionally well.

Four areas to eat in

Saint-Pierre

The old quarter near the river and the Place du Parlement is full of restaurants with terraces. Convenient and lively.

Saint-Michel

Around the Saint-Michel basilica, a mixed, multicultural neighbourhood with markets and more affordable places.

Chartrons

North along the river, the former wine merchants' quarter has wine bars, bistros and an antiques street.

Capucins and Victoire

Near the Marché des Capucins and the university area, casual counters and student-friendly prices.

Eating the French way

Lunch is often the main meal, and many restaurants offer a set menu (formule) at midday that is much cheaper than dinner. Restaurants usually serve lunch from around noon to two and dinner from around seven or later; outside those times, many kitchens are closed.

Beyond Bordeaux

The same habits work across France: eat your big meal at lunch, look for the formule, and order the region's own dishes rather than generic "French" food.

Common questions

What is entrecôte à la bordelaise?

Steak served with a red wine and shallot sauce, a Bordeaux classic.

What is a formule?

A set menu, often at lunch, that is usually cheaper than ordering à la carte.

Where do Bordeaux's oysters come from?

Mostly from nearby Arcachon Bay.
