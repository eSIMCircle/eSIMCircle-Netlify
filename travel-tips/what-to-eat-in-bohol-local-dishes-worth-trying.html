What to eat in Bohol: local dishes worth trying

Bohol's food mixes island seafood with local sweets and root crops. Panglao's beach restaurants are convenient, but Tagbilaran and the inland towns add more everyday flavour.

Six dishes to look for

Calamay

A sticky sweet made from glutinous rice, coconut milk and sugar, traditionally sold packed in coconut shells. It is Bohol's best-known food souvenir.

Peanut kisses

Small peanut and egg-white cookies shaped like the Chocolate Hills. Sold in packs across the island.

Kinampay and ube

Bohol is known for kinampay, a local purple yam variety, used in sweets and desserts alongside ube.

Broas

Light ladyfinger biscuits, another classic pasalubong, the Filipino tradition of bringing food gifts home.

Kinilaw

Raw fish cured with vinegar, citrus and aromatics, similar to ceviche. A natural island dish, best at places with very fresh fish.

Grilled seafood

Fish, squid and prawns, simply grilled, are easy to find on Panglao and along the coast.

Good to knowCalamay, peanut kisses and broas all travel well. Buy them as gifts on your last day.

Practical notes

Carry pesos in small notes for markets and local eateries. Bohol is spread out, so plan meals around the day's route rather than crossing the island for one restaurant.
