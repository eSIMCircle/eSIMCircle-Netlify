A Vietnam food guide for travelers, starting in Hoi An

Hoi An's food tells the story of a trading port: noodles, herbs, pork and dumplings carrying Chinese, Japanese and central Vietnamese influences, all in one small town.

eSIMCircle Editorial · Published November 8, 2025 · Updated December 27, 2025 · 3 min read

Four areas to eat in

The Ancient Town

The lantern-lit old town has restaurants, cafés and street stalls. Busy with visitors, but home to many of the dishes Hoi An is known for.

Cam Nam

Across the river on Cam Nam island, quieter restaurants serve local food, often with river views.

Cam Chau

East of the old town, residential Cam Chau has neighbourhood places where locals eat.

Tra Que

The vegetable village of Tra Que grows many of the herbs used in Hoi An's cooking. Some places offer farm meals and cooking classes.

Good to knowWhite rose dumplings are made by only a few families in Hoi An, which is why you rarely see them elsewhere.

Planning meals

Cao lầu and cơm gà make good lunches. Keep evenings for the riverside, street food and a bánh mì on the walk home.

Beyond Hoi An

Vietnam's food changes from north to south. Pho in Hanoi, bún bò in Huế and broken rice in Saigon each tell a regional story. Eat local wherever you go.

Common questions

What is cao lầu?

Thick, chewy noodles with pork, herbs and crisp toppings, the dish most associated with Hoi An.

What are white rose dumplings?

Translucent steamed dumplings with shrimp or pork, made by only a few families in Hoi An.

Where can I see where Hoi An's herbs are grown?

In Tra Que vegetable village, where some places offer farm meals and cooking classes.
